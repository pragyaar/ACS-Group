A practical review for the Digital Marketing Executive role

Good marketing starts with knowing what matters to the buyer.

ACS Group has a wide range of workplace services. I looked at how its public website explains those services, where the existing proof is strongest, and how I would turn one of those strengths into a focused campaign.

Prepared independently by a digital marketer applying for the role. This is a public-facing review and a campaign concept, not commissioned work or an audit of ACS's private analytics.
01 / What I noticed

The raw material is already there. The job is to make it useful to the right people.

I reviewed ACS Group's homepage, division pages, contact journey, sustainability pages and current job description. These are observations from public pages, not claims about internal performance.

01

The group offer is broad. Buyers need a clear starting point.

The homepage presents ACS as one partner across technology, design and essential supplies, then asks visitors to find their specialist. That breadth is a strength, but a new visitor may arrive with a specific problem rather than knowledge of the ACS divisions.

What I would do: Keep the division routes, but add a small “What are you trying to sort out?” path for common needs such as IT support, office purchasing, print and workspace changes. Route each answer to the relevant service page.

02

The sustainability work is specific enough to become useful content.

ACS's 2024 Sustainability Report says its Green List was reviewed internally and narrowed to 900 products that meet its higher threshold for environmentally friendly options. The report also describes customer environmental account reviews and the removal of listed single-use plastic items from its catalogue.

What I would do: Turn those facts into a practical guide for office and procurement teams. Explain what the Green List means, how the account review works, and what information a buyer should bring to a conversation. Keep the report year and definitions visible.

03

The website has customer proof, but it needs to match the question being asked.

The group homepage shows a range of customer names across public services, education and private business. That establishes breadth, but a logo alone does not tell a buyer what ACS delivered or what the process looked like.

What I would do: Work with account managers to build short case studies around one buyer problem, the service supplied, how delivery was handled and any outcome ACS can approve for publication. Do not add performance claims unless the customer has signed them off.

04

The contact page already separates enquiries by division.

It lists different contact details for Office, IT, Interiors and Print, and gives visitors different routes depending on whether they are an existing customer, a new customer or looking for support.

What I would do: Keep that useful routing. Then check the analytics and CRM to see which routes generate qualified enquiries, where people drop out and whether sales follow-up is consistent. The public page alone cannot tell us if conversion is good or poor.

What looks promising

The Green List, customer environmental reviews and sustainability reporting are concrete material. They give marketing something useful to explain, rather than relying on broad claims about being sustainable.

What I cannot tell from the outside

I cannot confirm search rankings, website conversion rates, paid media efficiency, email performance or lead quality without GA4, Search Console, advertising, email and CRM access. Those would be the first checks before setting targets.

02 / Campaign idea

Make sustainable office purchasing easier to understand.

I would start with ACS Office rather than try to promote every division at once. The public sustainability report provides a specific proof point, and the audience has a practical question: how can we review what we buy without making the process harder?

Proposed campaign platform

Better choices for the everyday things your team buys.

A plain-English guide to ACS Office's Green List and environmental account reviews. Useful first, promotional second.

Who it is for

Office managers, procurement teams and operations leads at organisations that regularly buy workplace supplies. Public-sector buyers may need a separate version with procurement requirements and evidence made clear.

The offer

A downloadable checklist: “Five things to review before changing your office supplies.” It would cover product requirements, current purchasing patterns, alternatives, reporting needs and who needs to approve changes.

Why this angle

It starts with a real buyer task and connects to information ACS already publishes. It also gives the sales team a useful reason to follow up, instead of sending a generic “get in touch” message.

Before launch

Confirm the current Green List count and criteria, which products can be promoted, the exact scope of environmental account reviews, and whether ACS has an existing downloadable guide. The 900-product figure is from the 2024 report and should not be presented as a current figure without checking.

Landing page copy · first draft

Could your office supplies work harder for your sustainability goals?

Small purchasing decisions add up. ACS Office has developed a Green List of products that meet its criteria for more environmentally friendly options, and customers can request an environmental review of their account.

This guide will help you review what your organisation buys, what matters to your team and what to ask before making changes.

Explore ACS Office

04 / Measurement

Measure whether the campaign creates useful conversations.

I would agree the definition of a qualified enquiry with sales before launch. Clicks and downloads are useful signals, but they are not the final result.

Primary measure

Qualified enquiries attributable to the campaign.

Efficiency

Cost per qualified enquiry, once spend and lead quality are known.

Quality check

Sales feedback on fit, timing and actual buying need.

I have not added forecast conversion rates, cost-per-lead targets or expected revenue. Those figures need ACS's historical results, budget, sales capacity and agreed definition of a qualified lead.

05 / First 90 days

Learn the business, test one thing properly, then scale what works.

This is how I would approach the role before making larger claims or committing more budget.

Days 1–30

Understand and baseline

  • Meet sales leads and subject-matter experts across divisions.
  • Review GA4, Search Console, CRM, email and paid media data.
  • Check tracking, consent, lead routing and reporting definitions.
  • Review current content and search competitors.
Days 31–60

Build and test

  • Choose one audience and one business goal with the team.
  • Publish or improve a focused landing page.
  • Test two messages or calls to action where traffic allows.
  • Brief sales on lead follow-up and feedback capture.
Days 61–90

Review and improve

  • Compare channel and audience performance.
  • Review qualified leads with sales, not just platform reports.
  • Improve the landing page, creative or targeting based on evidence.
  • Recommend whether to extend, change or stop the test.
